## The brief

Sustainable aviation fuel starts as biomass on farms scattered across Kalimantan. It has to be collected at depots, refined, and delivered to four airports, one of them supplied by barge. The question is where to build the depots and refineries so that 1,200 kilotonnes of fuel a year arrive at the lowest possible cost.

## The design

The landing opens on a night map of Kalimantan with the whole network drawn over it: farms in blue, depots and refineries in orange and green, and the routes between them. Beside it, one number: the delivered cost per tonne, broken into building, running and moving.

The planner behind it is a calm, light dashboard. The overview gives the cost, the facilities built and the truck trips. The network map, material flows and cost breakdown each answer a single question, and a scenario sandbox lets a planner switch on a flood, a new airport or new transport rates and re-run the optimiser.

> The cost shown is the proven lowest for the inputs, not an estimate. The screen says so in green.

## The details

- **Three legs, three colours.** Farm to depot is blue, depot to refinery orange, refinery to airport green, everywhere: the map, the routes list and the report.
- **Ask in plain words.** A command bar takes questions such as "A flood cuts West Kalimantan biomass by 30%. Is demand still feasible?"
- **Honest about what is outside the model.** The spare-biomass export plan and the live flights are labelled as illustrative and never change the fuel cost.
- **A report for the meeting.** One page with the map, the costs and every facility, ready to print or save as PDF.

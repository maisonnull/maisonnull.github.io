## The brief

A café owner has no time for charts. They want three answers about a trend they keep seeing online: is it coming here, can my kitchen make it, and when should I start? The interface had to answer those in the time it takes to pull an espresso shot.

## The design

The look comes from the shop, not from finance software. Fraunces sets the headlines like a menu board, Figtree keeps the numbers clear, and the colours are named after things on a hawker counter: pandan, kopi-o, chilli and gula melaka.

Each food gets one plain verdict at the top, with a single sentence explaining it. The chart sits underneath for anyone who wants it, never in front of the answer.

> Watch it rather than bet on a date.

When the evidence is uneven, the screen says so in words instead of showing a confident date. That honesty sets the tone of the whole interface: calm panels, soft status chips, and strong colour only when something is genuinely urgent.

## The details

- **A food, not a data point.** Every trend has its own small illustration, so a scan of the radar feels like reading a menu.
- **One question per screen.** Overview, opportunity, and launch board each answer one thing, so the owner can stop as soon as they have their answer.
- **The kitchen, named.** The "You can make it" check lists the equipment it relied on, so a wrong assumption is easy to spot.
- **Dark appearance.** Every colour was re-tuned for a dark room rather than inverted.

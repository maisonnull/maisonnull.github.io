## The brief

Family recipes often live only in someone's head, written as "a pinch" or "until it smells right". The person who knows the recipe is usually older, standing at a stove with flour on their hands. The interface had to work for her from across the kitchen, and the result had to feel like something worth keeping.

## The design

Pinch looks like a recipe notebook, not an app. Recipes are set in Lora on warm paper, with a notebook margin line. Her own words appear in Caveat, a handwritten face, beside the step they belong to, so the card reads in her voice.

While cooking, the screen shows one short question at a time in very large type on a dark rosemary panel, readable from a metre away. Most of the time it stays quiet.

> The butter amount was never said, so it stays unknown.

Anything she didn't actually say is marked "amount unknown" instead of guessed, and the card ends with a "Still unknown" list. A gap becomes a reason to call her again, not a silent error.

## The details

- **One colour that means "go".** Paprika is used only for the main action, so there is never doubt about what to press.
- **Readable from the stove.** Large serif type, high contrast, and one idea per screen.
- **A card worth printing.** Warm paper tones and a clean print layout, because the result is meant to live in a drawer, not just an app.
